Property goes unclaimed most often because contact information changes and the organization holding your funds is unable to reach you. Common types include:

  • Bank accounts (savings, checking, CDs)
  • Unredeemed money orders or gift cards
  • Uncashed dividend, payroll, or court-ordered checks
  • Estate proceeds and trust distributions
  • Insurance benefits or policies
  • Stocks, bonds, and mutual funds
  • Telephone, utility, or security deposits
  • Customer overpayments and mineral royalty payments
  • Annuities and pension distributions

In most cases, your funds remain yours regardless of how long they've been held — but it's best to act promptly, as some states impose time limits after property is transferred to the state. Here's how the process works:

1. Pre-Escheatment (Est. release: 6–8 weeks)

Unclaimed property begins as an outstanding account payable. When an organization cannot locate the rightful owner, all 50 states require them to report and transfer the funds to the state — a process called escheatment.

2. Escheatment

The formal transfer of unclaimed property to the state. Accounts with no activity for a defined dormancy period are classified as abandoned or unclaimed.

3. Post-Escheatment (Avg. return: 16 weeks)

After the state takes custody, it may liquidate certain assets and hold the cash equivalent. If your claim is approved, the state reimburses you at the property's value at the time of transfer.
